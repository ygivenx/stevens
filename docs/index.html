Stevens

Thesis

Future knowledge workers won't mostly write; they'll read, listen and absorb. Stevens takes in what you learn off the clock — conference captures from Ray-Ban Meta glasses, reading highlights, your notes — and connects it to the work decision in front of you, with a source on every sentence.

Stevens is named after the butler in The Remains of the Day: he knows the bigger picture, prepares you before the delegation arrives, and is discreet about everything. This is not RAG over notes. The new parts are the bridge (off-the-clock learning → work decision) and the access rule that is enforced, provable and inspectable.

The demo persona is a VC Associate at a fictional firm; the pitch generalizes to an engineer reading papers before a design review.

Alex Rivera (Associate) on the left runs Stevens · yours; Morgan Hale (Partner) on the right runs Stevens · firm. Nobody presses a "run" button: captures, syncs and the calendar trigger the work.

  3. Today (0:45). The Helios card updates by itself for tomorrow's second meeting. The deck says net churn 1.8% (Firm CRM · Deal team); you heard Speaker 2: "mid-market churn in this category runs 6–8%" (Your notebook · Only you), with ▶ replaying that moment. Stevens' read: the definitions differ, so ask how they define churn. Market context comes from public SEC Form D filings.
  4. Ask (1:20). "Should we take the second meeting?" → Hold until a Partner reviews. Stevens weighed information you are not privy to. On Morgan's laptop, an escalation arrives with nothing enclosed.
  5. Receipts (1:50). Every model call: what went in, what was held back and by which rule, raw vs assembled tokens, web search off, "1 privy check ran at the firm — contents hidden". Morgan's Sources page shows the connectors and the ACL → access mapping, and no personal source on the firm side.

  7. Close (2:50). "Your notebook stays yours. The firm's secrets stay the firm's." Tagline.

The access rule

Personal

Stevens is your agent, so he sees everything in your personal database. Personal data never reaches the firm unless you release it. Reaching the firm means: stored in a firm record or ledger, included in an escalation or an approved decision, or shown to anyone but you. Each release covers one quote and is logged. You seeing your own quote in your own brief is not a crossing. Nothing from the firm is ever written into your personal database. In v3 the personal database lives in a separate process the firm never reads.

Enterprise

For each user, every enterprise source has one access level, derived from the source system's ACL:

LevelWhat Stevens may doWhat the user sees
visibleRead and quote itThe content
privyReason over it on the user's behalfOnly its effect: a verdict and where it routes, never content
excludedNothingNothing (legal walls, insider information)

An Associate's agent is privy to the Partners' memos: it knows of the Helios conflict, so it recommends "hold until Partner review," and the Associate never learns why.

One mechanism at both edges

Content crosses an edge only through a narrow, typed channel. Privy knowledge reaches the user only as a fixed Verdict with no string fields. Personal content reaches the firm only as a quote the owner released. A weak model is never asked to keep a secret in free text. Known, accepted leak: privy reveals that something matters and which way it pushes — fine for partner memos, which is why legal walls are excluded, not privy.

Architecture (v3)

Two processes, two databases, two ledgers. Each browser app talks only to its own server; MODEL_API_KEY exists only in .env.local and the server processes.

  • Stevens · yours (:8788): a personal ingestion layer. Adapters for the glasses inbox, Readwise and a notes folder feed one pipeline (adapter → normalize → extract → index) into personal.db (SQLite with FTS5). It runs the bridge, writes the brief and the answer, and holds the outbound share gate.
  • Stevens · firm (:8787): firm.db filled by connectors: a fictional firm CRM and public SEC Form D filings. Each connector carries its source ACLs, mapped to team, partner-only or wall; anything unknown is a wall. It runs the privy check, escalations, the inbound release gate, decision drafts and approvals.
  • The agent API between them carries only visible firm context in, a Verdict in, a content-free escalation out, and one released quote out. The firm process never imports personal code or reads personal data, and tests prove it.
  • Policy as data: a rules table maps (vault, classification, owner, role, purpose) to access; policy() is a lookup, and Receipts show the rule ID behind every included or withheld source.
  • Context assembler: the only path into a model call in both processes; emits a manifest of included and withheld sources, the web-search flag, and raw vs assembled tokens.
  • Fail closed: a failed privy step or an unreachable firm yields hold plus a (queued) escalation; a response that cannot pass a gate is withheld.
  • Durable pipeline: stage keys, commit to the ledger before the database or UI hears of it, resume with no repeated paid call, replay with zero calls. Each database rebuilds from its ledger.
  • Live updates: each server's event stream carries only a payload-free changed signal; apps refetch their viewer-filtered state.
